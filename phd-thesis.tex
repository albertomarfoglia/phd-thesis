\begin{abstract}
The increasing availability of healthcare data and advances in Artificial Intelligence (AI) have created new opportunities for clinical prediction and decision support. However, scientific progress should serve the public good and respect fundamental rights and democratic values. Trust in AI cannot be assumed; systems must remain open to scrutiny to earn it. Achieving this requires the ability to inspect how data are represented, how their meaning is made explicit, and how they are bound to the models that consume them. This thesis investigates how semantic, data-centric engineering can make these dependencies explicit through machine-actionable specifications, thereby supporting the requirements of Trustworthy AI.

The research addresses this objective through three complementary methodological stages, each investigated separately. (i) \emph{Harmonization and Structuring} examines dependencies between source data and standardized representations. A systematic review identifies barriers to adopting health data standards, while a modular FHIR-driven pipeline uses declarative mappings and automated validation to ensure transparent, verifiable transformations. (ii) \emph{Semantic Enrichment} makes data semantics and model requirements explicit. MEDS-OWL represents clinical events and provenance, the Manifest Protocol formalizes AI model inputs and outputs, and graph-based prediction experiments assess the impact of semantic representations on predictive performance. (iii) \emph{Inference and Reasoning} addresses dependencies between model requirements and available data. CONNECTED uses semantic reasoning to match model requirements to Digital Twin data and enable automated execution.

The results demonstrate the feasibility of the proposed strategies across all three stages, highlighting their potential to systematize and automate the transformation of heterogeneous data into actionable knowledge. Finally, integrating data-centric and semantic perspectives complements model-centric AI development and lays the groundwork for trustworthy AI systems.
\end{abstract}
